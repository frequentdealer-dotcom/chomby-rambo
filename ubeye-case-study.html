FD Innovations / Project Retrospective / 001

UBEYE.

An online barbershop venture that reached paying customers but struggled to establish the financial, managerial and operational foundations necessary for continuity.

Business Development Brand Identity Project Retrospective
01 / INTRODUCTION

A Business Idea With Potential

UBEYE Transformation was conceived as an online barbershop targeting students around the University of Lusaka campus in the Leopards Hill area.

The concept sought to connect customers with barbering services through an appointment-based arrangement. Rather than relying solely on walk-in customers, the venture intended to use social media to create awareness, attract bookings and establish a recognisable brand.

The project was originated by a colleague of the founder's circle. The founder developed the business idea, while a colleague was to provide the barbering service.

Business Concept

Online barbershop

Target Market

Students around UNILUS, Leopards Hill

Service Model

Appointment-based barbering services

Marketing Channel

Social media, particularly Instagram

02 / THE ORIGINAL MODEL

How UBEYE Was Supposed to Work

The venture relied on an appointment-based arrangement. Customers would be attracted through social media and arrange barbering services through the business.

01. Awareness

Social media posts introduced UBEYE to its target market.

02. Enquiry

Interested students would make contact and enquire about services.

03. Appointment

Customers would arrange a suitable appointment.

04. Service Delivery

The participating barber would provide the barbering service.

The critical dependency: UBEYE depended on the continued participation of its barber. The availability and commitment of this individual were therefore central to the operation of the venture.

04 / MARKET ENTRY

UBEYE Reached Real Customers

UBEYE was not merely a concept that remained on paper. The venture reached the market, attracted paying customers and provided barbering services for which customers paid.

Social media, particularly Instagram, was the principal promotional channel identified during the project.

UBEYE demonstrated that it could attract paying customers. The challenge was sustaining the business behind the service.

05 / THE DECLINE

When the Business Could Not Continue

Despite reaching paying customers, UBEYE encountered difficulties that affected its ability to continue operating.

Inadequate Business Planning

In retrospect, the project lacked a sufficiently developed business plan and a clearly documented framework for managing the venture.

No Defined Budgets

According to the project contributor's assessment, budgets were not established to guide expenditure, service costs and the financial requirements of operating the business.

Difficulty Compensating the Barber

The business struggled to pay its barber. The barber also regarded participation in UBEYE as a side hustle, rather than a primary business commitment.

Loss of the Barber

The barber eventually left the venture. According to the account provided, he departed with UBEYE's machines.

Business Closure

Following the barber's departure, no attempt was made to restart operations, replace the barber or recover the equipment. The venture did not resume operations.

06 / WHY IT FAILED

Understanding the Underlying Problems

The following findings are based on the observations of the person who contributed to the project. They represent a retrospective assessment rather than an independent financial or operational audit.

01 / Strategic planning

Absence of a Defined Business Plan

The venture lacked a sufficiently developed plan setting out its objectives, operational structure, financial requirements and approach to sustainable growth.

Without a clear plan, the project had no established framework against which its progress could be assessed.

02 / Financial management

Inadequate Budgeting

No budgets were established to guide the financial operations of the venture. This contributed to a situation in which the business could not adequately compensate its barber.

The difficulty in meeting this essential operational cost exposed a weakness in the financial foundation of the business.

03 / Human resources

Dependence on One Service Provider

UBEYE relied on a single barber whose participation was essential to delivering the service. His involvement was treated as a side hustle, creating a potential mismatch between the commitment required by the business and the commitment available.

04 / Governance

Absence of Written Arrangements

The parties did not establish written arrangements clearly defining their respective responsibilities, financial contributions, compensation and the management of business property.

This left important matters dependent on informal understandings rather than documented terms.

05 / Asset management

Unclear Equipment Ownership and Custody

Following the barber's departure, he reportedly left with UBEYE's machines. The FD contributor only became aware of this after the fallout.

The circumstances surrounding the acquisition and ownership of the machines were not known to the contributor. It is therefore not possible to establish who owned them or what arrangements governed their use.

06 / Project management

No Structured Exit or Continuity Arrangements

The venture had no documented arrangements identified for what would happen if its essential service provider withdrew. Once the barber departed, the business did not resume operations.

07 / FD'S OWN EXPERIENCE

A Lesson in Commercial Engagements

The UBEYE experience also exposed shortcomings in how FD Innovations approached its own business engagements.

FD contributed to the development of the UBEYE brand and its promotional presence. However, there was no formal arrangement establishing payment, commission, equity participation or deferred compensation for the work.

The contributor confirms that FD received no payment from the venture for its creative and promotional work.

Goodwill is not a substitute for a commercial agreement.

What FD Should Have Done Differently

Define the Engagement

Establish whether FD is acting as a service provider, business partner, investor or collaborator.

Agree on Compensation

Establish fees, commission or other consideration before creative work begins.

Document the Scope

Clearly identify the work to be undertaken, deliverables, responsibilities and expected timelines.

Maintain Project Records

Keep records of work undertaken, approvals, revisions, payments and outstanding obligations.

08 / LESSONS LEARNED

What UBEYE Taught Us

01. A Business Needs More Than a Good Idea

A commercially attractive concept requires planning, financial preparation and an operational structure capable of supporting its activities.

02. Revenue Does Not Automatically Mean Sustainability

Attracting paying customers is only one part of running a business. The venture must also meet its costs, compensate its service providers and maintain operations.

03. People Are Critical Business Resources

A business that depends on one individual must establish clear expectations, compensation arrangements and continuity measures.

04. Business Assets Must Be Properly Documented

Ownership, custody and use of equipment should be recorded. Written arrangements help reduce uncertainty when relationships change or a participant leaves.

05. Creative Work Has Commercial Value

Design, branding and promotional services require time and resources. FD must establish commercial terms before undertaking work for other ventures.

06. Small Businesses Need Basic Formal Systems

Even a small venture should have basic records, financial controls, written agreements and a clear allocation of responsibilities.

09 / THE WAY FORWARD

How This Experience Shapes FD Innovations

UBEYE provides a practical reference point for how FD Innovations approaches future projects and client engagements.

Formal Engagements

Written scopes of work and clear commercial terms.

Project Planning

Defined objectives, deliverables, budgets and timelines.

Financial Discipline

Clear fees, payment milestones and expenditure planning.

Accountability

Documented responsibilities, approvals and project records.

UBEYE did not become the sustainable venture originally envisioned. Nevertheless, the experience provides valuable lessons for FD Innovations as it develops its own services, products and future client relationships.

10 / FINAL REFLECTION

Beyond the Outcome

UBEYE was an attempt to turn an idea into a functioning business. It reached customers, generated paid transactions and established a visual identity.

Its eventual collapse illustrates the importance of connecting a business idea with financial planning, reliable operational arrangements and proper governance.

For FD Innovations, the experience also marked an important lesson in recognising the value of its creative services and protecting its commercial interests.

Not every project becomes a success story. Some become lessons that shape the next one.

This retrospective is based on the account and observations of an individual who contributed to UBEYE's creative development. It is not an independently audited account of the venture. Certain matters, including equipment ownership and the precise financial history, remain unverified. The account may be supplemented with further records and perspectives.
